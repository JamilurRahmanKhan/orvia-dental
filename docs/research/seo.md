## 3. Local SEO ranking factors (BrightLocal / Whitespark)

- BrightLocal's 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors survey (published/updated September 2026) weights: **on-page optimization 24%, Google Business Profile signals 17%, link signals 15%, review signals 14% (up from 16% in 2023 by one framing / 20% in another), citation signals 9%, behavioral 8%, personalization 8%, social 6%.** Exact split varies slightly by source cut (local pack vs local organic vs AI search), but GBP + on-page + reviews together dominate. [BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/google-local-algorithm-and-ranking-factors/)
- Whitespark's 2026 survey (47 experts, 187 factors) puts the three heaviest categories as **GBP signals (32%), review signals (20%), on-page signals (15%)**. [Whitespark](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/)
- Top individual GBP factors: primary category selection, proximity to searcher, keywords in business title. [BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/google-local-algorithm-and-ranking-factors/)
- Review recency is a top 2026 factor — **74% of users look for reviews from the last 3 months**; quantity, star average, recency, keywords in review text, and owner response behavior all matter. [Whitespark](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/)
- NAP (Name/Address/Phone) consistency across GBP, website, and directories remains critical; current opening hours (incl. holidays) and accurate attributes (accessible, parking, wifi) increase trust. [Whitespark](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/)
- **Implication for Orvia**: the website's job is not to "win SEO alone" — it must feed and match a well-maintained Google Business Profile (identical NAP, service list, categories) and actively drive fresh reviews with a request flow, since review recency/velocity is now a top-tier ranking factor.

## 4. Structured data / schema.org

- **Dentist** is a valid schema.org type, extending `MedicalBusiness` (which extends `LocalBusiness`) and `MedicalOrganization`. It inherits LocalBusiness properties (opening hours, payment methods accepted, price range, address/geo, telephone) plus MedicalOrganization properties (`isAcceptingNewPatients`, `medicalSpecialty`, `healthPlanNetworkId`). [schema.org/Dentist](https://schema.org/Dentist)
- Recommended layered markup for a dental site: `Dentist`/`MedicalBusiness` on the homepage and location page, `MedicalProcedure` on individual treatment pages (implants, Invisalign, root canal, etc.), `Person`/`Physician` for each dentist bio with credentials, `AggregateRating` fed by a verified third-party source. [PracticeRank](https://practicerank.ai/blog/schema-markup-for-dentists/)
- **FAQPage rich results are effectively dead**: Google restricted FAQ rich results to "well-known, authoritative government and health websites" in August 2023, then removed FAQ rich results from Search entirely on **May 7, 2026**, with the FAQ filter/report/Rich Results Test support being removed June 2026 and Search Console API support ending August 2026. Google gave no public rationale. FAQ schema is still valid to keep (it doesn't hurt, and may help AI search systems parse content) but will **not** produce a visible SERP snippet anymore — do not build the content strategy around winning FAQ rich results. [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-drops-faq-rich-results-from-search/574429/)
- **Review/AggregateRating snippet restriction**: Google is explicit that if a business "controls the reviews about itself," pages using `LocalBusiness`/`Organization` schema are **ineligible** for the star-rating rich result — this includes reviews collected via an on-site form or an embedded third-party widget (e.g., an embedded Google or Facebook reviews widget) placed on the business's own homepage. Ratings must be sourced directly from users, not curated by human editors. **Practical consequence for Orvia**: do not mark up an on-site "Rate Us" testimonial block with `AggregateRating`/`Review` schema expecting stars in search — that markup should point to genuinely independent, user-sourced ratings (e.g., syndicated Google review data via an approved widget/API), or simply be left off and reviews displayed as unmarked UI content. [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/review-snippet)

## 5. Content types that rank

- **Treatment/service pages** (one per procedure: implants, Invisalign, root canal, whitening, veneers, emergency, sedation, pediatric) — table stakes; every competitor has these.
- **Cost/pricing pages**, often nested under the service (`/dental-implants/cost/`) rather than folded into the general service copy — this is a distinct, high-intent URL pattern that ranks separately and should be replicated. [myaustindds.com](https://www.myaustindds.com/services/dental-implants/cost/)
- **Insurance/payer pages** (Medicaid, PPO, CHIP, financing) as their own URLs, not a paragraph on a general "New Patients" page. [themeadowsdentalcare.com](https://themeadowsdentalcare.com/insurance/medicaid)
- **Emergency page** as a standalone, always-linked-from-header page with hours/same-day promise — the highest urgency, highest-converting page type in the niche. [southaustindentalassociates.com](https://southaustindentalassociates.com/services/emergency-dentist-austin-tx/)
- **Neighborhood/location pages**: recommended practice from dental SEO agencies is that a *few* genuinely differentiated neighborhood pages (with real local context — parking, transit, patient patterns) outperform many thin duplicated ones; avoid template-swap city pages. [Growth Saloon](https://www.growthsaloon.com/industries/seo-for-dentists/)
- **Long-form guides** (e.g., "everything about dental implants: candidacy, procedure, cost, recovery") are increasingly important because AI Overviews and AI chat search synthesize from comprehensive pages rather than short keyword-targeted articles. [DentalScapes](https://www.dentalscapes.com/seo-for-dentists-adapting-to-ai-search/)

## 6. AI Overviews / AI search impact

- AI Overviews now appear on an estimated **~68% of local searches** generally, and **~30%+ of dental-specific queries**; a WebFX analysis of 130K+ health searches found AI Overviews on ~51% of them. [omnifunnelmarketing.com](https://www.omnifunnelmarketing.com/blog/how-dental-practices-can-dominate-local-search-with-ai-enhanced-seo), [dentalscapes.com](https://www.dentalscapes.com/seo-for-dentists-adapting-to-ai-search/)
- AI search tools (Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity) are cited as influencing roughly **67% of dental patient research** before choosing a provider — appearing in AI answers is becoming as important as classic page-1 ranking. [FirmCite](https://www.firmcite.com/blog/marketing-dental-practice)
- Traditional local SEO still reportedly drives **~43% of new patient acquisition**, but agencies now frame it as needing to be paired with AI-search optimization (comprehensive answer-style content, strong GBP data feeding AI answers, clean structured data) rather than treated as separate. [omnifunnelmarketing.com](https://www.omnifunnelmarketing.com/blog/how-dental-practices-can-dominate-local-search-with-ai-enhanced-seo)
- **Implication**: write treatment/cost pages as complete, self-contained answers (what it is, candidacy, procedure steps, real cost range, recovery, insurance) rather than short marketing blurbs — this format is what both classic SEO and AI-answer synthesis reward.

## 7. Core Web Vitals

- Core Web Vitals are a confirmed but secondary Google ranking signal (part of page experience since June 2021) — they act more as a **tie-breaker/filter** among pages of comparable content quality than a primary ranking driver; content quality, relevance, and links still dominate. [minddwave.com](https://minddwave.com/blog/core-web-vitals-2026/)
- Stricter CWV thresholds took effect **January 2026**. Following the March 2026 core update, position-1 pages show a **10% higher CWV pass rate** than position-9 pages for the same query, indicating CWV increasingly correlates with — even if it doesn't single-handedly cause — top rankings. [rivuletiq.com](https://www.rivuletiq.com/core-web-vitals-2026-whats-changed-and-how-to-pass/)
- Mobile carries the most weight: **64%+ of global traffic is mobile** and Google is mobile-first indexed; a page only earns a "good" CWV rating when all three metrics (LCP, INP, CLS) are good for at least 75% of real visits per Chrome UX Report data. [deftsoft.com](https://deftsoft.com/core-web-vitals-2026-how-page-speed-affects-your-google-rankings/)
- **Implication for build**: optimize hero imagery (treatment photos, team photos) aggressively (modern formats, responsive sizes, no layout shift from web fonts/ads), keep interaction latency low on booking/contact forms, and test at 375/768/1280/1920 as per house standard — this doubles as both UX and a measurable ranking input.

## 8. Gap: what competitors DO vs. what patients WANT

| Competitors do | Patients want (evidence) | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| List services with vague "call for pricing" | Real, specific price ranges up front — pages that rank best for cost queries give actual $ ranges (e.g., "$3,000-$7,000 single implant") | Orvia should publish real cost ranges on dedicated cost pages, not hide pricing behind a call |
| Bury insurance/Medicaid info in a general "New Patients" page | Patients search "dentist that takes medicaid austin" as a distinct, high-friction query | Standalone, clearly labeled insurance/Medicaid page needed, linked from main nav |
| Generic "Emergency Dentistry" paragraph on a services page | Patients in pain search "emergency dentist near me" wanting same-day/walk-in confirmation instantly | Standalone emergency page, linked in header/sticky mobile bar, with clear hours and urgency language |
| On-site "leave us a review" widgets marked up with review schema hoping for stars | Google explicitly disallows self-served AggregateRating stars for LocalBusiness — most competitors likely don't realize this markup is invisible in SERPs | Don't waste engineering effort on this; invest instead in a genuine review-generation flow (SMS/email post-visit request) that grows real, recent Google reviews, since review recency/velocity is a top ranking factor |
| Template-swapped "dentist in [neighborhood]" pages with duplicated copy | Local searchers respond to genuine local specificity (parking, transit, community references) | Build 2-3 real neighborhood pages with unique local content rather than many thin ones |
